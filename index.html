00 · QUESTION

What gets lost when public infrastructure is automated?

How do automated systems, presented as neutral tools of progress, reshape public space, ritual and social relations?

Automation is usually presented as a neutral upgrade. This project asks what it quietly removes from the places where the most private parts of life meet public order: the cemetery and the public toilet.

Over three terms I built two speculative scenarios, presented them the way efficiency is sold, and showed a working machine to the public at the RCA Graduate Show 2025.

The scenarios suggest that automation erases acts rather than objects, that people answer its gaps with stories, and that public services run for efficiency start to sort access into tiers.

Q1

How do automated systems, presented as neutral tools of progress, embed political, ethical and economic logics that reconfigure public space, ritual and social relations?

Q2

What is the “ghostly remainder” that escapes machinic analysis, and how can speculative design make it palpable?

Q3

How can aesthetic shock, mimicry and exaggeration move an audience from passive consumption to critical inquiry?

01 · CONTEXT AND METHOD

Counter-automation as a method

Automation is sold as an inevitable march towards efficiency and convenience. Counter-Automation does not reject it. It pushes the logic to its end point, in a cemetery and a public toilet, until what gets lost becomes visible.

Cemeteries and public toilets are where the most private parts of life, death and the body, meet publicness, discipline and social order.

Each scenario is presented the way efficiency is usually sold: product renders, campaign posters, a launch film, a working machine.

Question. Does automation standardise and reduce the human experience, or does it infinitely expand it?

Method. Speculative world-building, expert interviews, prototyping, and a framework reading Stiegler and Arendt against Pinker and Kurzweil.

01

A critical design and research method informed by Accelerationist thought. It interrogates the hidden structures and power relations embedded within automation.

02

A framework for examining how automation reshapes public space, labour and human agency, often masked by narratives of efficiency and neutrality.

03

A challenge to the dominant ideology that technological adoption automatically leads to societal improvement.

02 · SCENARIO 1 · LONDON 2024–25

The Automated Gravestone

Speculative film, 2 min 8 s. The interviewees are fictional characters created for the scenario.

What this scenario tests

Can a cemetery be run like a machine? Removing offerings on a timer tests whether automation clears away only objects, or the act of placing them.

Cemeteries are where public order and private grief collide. The Automated Gravestone collects whatever is placed on it after the visitor leaves and restores its surface. The grave is always clean. What disappears is the act of placing something there.

Exhibition wall text

Welcome to the Automated Gravestone. This system does not retain memory or meaning. It removes objects, not grief.

Public cemeteries are filled with unclassified grief and unsanctioned offerings. The Automated Gravestone converts disorder into order. All are cleared on schedule.

It only processes material. It does not ask why something was left. Simply decides when to take it away.

Interviews with a political economist and a UX designer tested how plausible the system was. I then built it as a working machine and shot it in a real cemetery.

02.03

Expert interviews

Political economist, King’s College London

Treated as a facility upgrade, the system fits existing cemetery infrastructure; the decision sits with owners as capital investment. In public cemeteries the question is who pays. In private ones it points to tiered service.

“Premium options will emerge. Some people will be able to buy longer-lasting memorials, while others won’t.”

Ux designer, hci

Automation’s side effect is emotional misalignment. Her fix was customisation: let people set the level of automation and keep the final decision.

I chose the opposite path and removed choice. Visitors confront a gravestone that refuses to remember: Avery Gordon’s ghostly remainder.

03 · SCENARIO 2 · 2025

LOOLOO, the Automated Toilet

What this scenario tests

What happens to a public service, and to privacy, when it is run for efficiency and advertising revenue?

Scan a QR code and facial recognition opens the door. Stay too long and the walls slowly turn transparent. Watch an ad for more time, or subscribe. Privacy becomes a service.

1 / 2

The problem · case study: Sanisettes in Paris, run by an advertising company since the 1980s

Paris tried automated Sanisettes in the 1980s. The machines worked, but people did not behave predictably, and units were shut when someone stayed too long. LOOLOO answers with “truly complete automation”.

Pitch

This is a toilet. But not just any toilet. This is a fully automated public toilet.

We respect your time. But also, we respect the time of the person knocking on the door.

Want more time? No problem! Just watch an ad.

04 · FINDINGS

What the scenarios showed

Finding 1

Automation removes acts, not objects

The Automated Gravestone clears offerings on a schedule, so nothing stays on the grave for long.

A UK cemetery felt strangely different from the ones I knew in South Korea. It was not the object disappearing that troubled people. It was the act of letting it go.

Finding 2

People fill the gaps technology leaves with stories

Visitors did not only read the machine as loss. One imagined her offerings being transferred to the other side.

This is what the essay calls the ghostly remainder, after Avery Gordon: what escapes machinic processing, and returns as meaning.

Finding 3

Efficient public services sort access into tiers

In LOOLOO, extra time is bought by watching an ad or by subscribing. Privacy becomes a service.

The expert interviews pointed the same way for cemeteries: in public ones the question is who pays, in private ones it leads to tiered service.

05 · FINAL ESSAY · AN ETHICS OF THE REMAINDER

Ghostly Matters

The machine does not make mistakes; if there is an error, it is on the human side. The cemetery is clean, the toilet is precise, and the disorderly traces of humanity are collected like artifacts. What remains are the questions technology cannot process.

Argument

Four parts

PARTSUMMARY
01 PoliticsTechnology is a political decision. Winner’s low overpasses kept bus users from the beaches; Stiegler warns automation subsumes the senses; Arendt locates freedom in Action, the unpredictable beginnings automation risks erasing.
02 PracticePush the logic of automation to its limit so its contradictions show. After Pater and Berger the work imitates the graphics that justify automation; after Dunne and Raby it aims for an aesthetic shock.
03 ScenariosThe gravestone erases the ritual gesture to resolve tidiness against mourning. LOOLOO turns privacy into a timed, ad-funded service.
04 MovementDe Certeau’s tactics and Merleau-Ponty’s moving body: unpredictable movement and creative misuse open room for resistance.

06 · LIMITS

Limitations and next steps

Speculative, not deployed

The Automated Gravestone was built as a working machine, filmed in a cemetery and shown at the RCA Graduate Show; it has not been installed in a public cemetery. LOOLOO exists as a service scenario, renders and film.

A small evidence base

Three expert interviews, with a political economist, a UX designer and an architect, and visitor responses from the graduate show.

Next

Developing the next gravestone with an engineer collaborator, and installing it in a public setting where it can meet mourners rather than exhibition visitors.

Account for the surplus
